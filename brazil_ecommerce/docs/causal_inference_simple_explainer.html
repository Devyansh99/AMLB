Causal Inference Notebook: Simple Visual Explanation

Main question: Does long shipping distance actually cause more unhappy customers, or is it only connected to other factors like heavy products, high freight, and seller speed?

Notebook Goal

Cause vs Correlation

  • Correlation says two things move together.
  • Causation says one thing directly changes the other.
  • This notebook tries to estimate a fair causal effect of shipping distance.
1Load and prepare data
2Build treatment and outcome
3Control confounders
4Estimate ATE and test robustness

Key Variables

  • Outcome: is_dissatisfied (1 if review score < 4)
  • Treatment: long_distance (1 if distance is above median)
  • Confounders: weight, price, freight, seller speed, seller size, items, north indicator

What Confounder Means

A confounder is a variable that affects both distance and dissatisfaction. If you ignore it, your distance effect can be biased.

Example: Heavy products may travel farther and also create more complaints. Without adjustment, distance may look worse than it really is.

How ATE Is Estimated

  • Method 1: Backdoor linear regression
  • Method 2: Propensity score matching (PSM)
ATE = average(Outcome if Long Distance) - average(Outcome if Short Distance), after adjustment

Notebook interpretation example: around +1.52 percentage points, meaning long distance likely increases dissatisfaction by about 1.52 points on average.

Refutation Check 1

Random Common Cause: adds random noise confounder and checks if estimate collapses.

Refutation Check 2

Placebo Treatment: uses fake treatment and expects near-zero effect.

Sensitivity Check

Tests how hidden confounding strength would change your estimated effect.

Version 2 (Advanced Part)

flowchart TD A[Use continuous distance_km] --> B[Fit CausalForestDML] B --> C[Estimate effect per +1 km and +100 km] C --> D[Find subgroup effects by state and product category] D --> E[Run hidden-confounding sensitivity grid]

This improves realism because distance is not truly just short vs long. It also helps identify where the effect is strongest.

Simple Business Meaning

  • Distance appears to have a real negative impact on customer satisfaction.
  • Operational actions: reduce long-distance pain with faster prep, better packaging, stronger regional logistics.
  • This is still an estimate, not absolute truth, because hidden confounders may exist.
